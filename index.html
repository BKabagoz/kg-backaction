Why this matters

In a precision optical measurement, the same light that measures a mechanical degree of freedom also perturbs it. Shot noise limits measurement precision, while optical amplitude fluctuations exert fluctuating radiation-pressure forces. These form the imprecision and back-action components of quantum noise.

Frequency-dependent squeezing provides a way to engineer correlations between these fluctuations and improve detector sensitivity. In this experiment, those correlations are used not only to reduce total quantum noise, but also to reshape the effective quantum reservoir seen by LIGO's differential arm mode and suppress quantum back-action-driven motion of a feedback-induced optomechanical mode at 151 Hz with an effective mass of approximately 10 kg, a quality factor of approximately 100.

To estimate the individual contributions from measurement imprecision, radiation-pressure back-action, and their correlations in a multiport interferometer such as LIGO, we developed a platform-agnostic method for decomposing the measured quantum noise into these three contributions.

Central result. Around the feedback-induced resonance, frequency-dependent squeezing reduces displacement-noise power by approximately 47% relative to no squeezing and by approximately 31% relative to frequency-independent squeezing, while retaining comparable high-frequency measurement imprecision. The correlation-driven back-action-evasion metric reaches approximately 70% near resonance.
